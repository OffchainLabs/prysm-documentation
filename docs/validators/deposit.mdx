Your validator exists once its ETH is deposited into the **deposit contract** together with your deposit data.

<When net="mainnet">

Use the official **Staking Launchpad**: it checks your `deposit_data-*.json` file and sends the transaction from your wallet.

</When>
<When net="hoodi">

Use the official **EthStaker console**: it checks your `deposit_data-*.json` file and sends the transaction from your wallet.

## Before you begin

<When net="mainnet">

- Have your `deposit_data-*.json` file. See [Create validator keys](/validators/create-keys/).
- Have your node ready: execution client and beacon node synced, keys imported, fee recipient set. A validator that activates while your node is down loses balance. See [Import keys](/validators/import-keys/).
- Have a wallet (browser extension or hardware wallet) with the deposit amount plus gas.

</When>
<When net="hoodi">

- Have your `deposit_data-*.json` file. See [Create validator keys](/validators/create-keys/).
- Have your node ready: execution client and beacon node synced, keys imported, fee recipient set. A validator that activates while your node is down loses balance. See [Import keys](/validators/import-keys/).
- Have a wallet (browser extension or hardware wallet) with the deposit amount plus gas. Get Hoodi ETH for free from a faucet, such as the [Google Cloud Hoodi faucet](https://cloud.google.com/application/web3/faucet/ethereum/hoodi) or the [pk910 Hoodi PoW faucet](https://hoodi-faucet.pk910.de/). A faucet only needs an address: never give one your mnemonic or private key.

</When>

<When net="mainnet">

:::danger[Only use the official launchpad]
Scam sites copy the launchpad and send your deposit to their own contract. Type the address yourself or use a bookmark; never follow links from ads, search results, chats or emails.

- **Launchpad:** <a href={mainnet.launchpad}>{mainnet.launchpad}</a>
- **Deposit contract:** <code>{mainnet.depositContract}</code>

Before you sign, check that the transaction goes to exactly this contract address. You can compare it with [ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/en/staking/deposit-contract/).
:::

</When>
<When net="hoodi">

:::danger[Only use the official console]
Scam sites copy the console and send your deposit to their own contract. Type the address yourself or use a bookmark; never follow links from ads, search results, chats or emails.

- **Deposit page:** [https://hoodi.console.ethstaker.org/deposit](https://hoodi.console.ethstaker.org/deposit)
:::

</When>

Never send ETH to the deposit contract with a plain transfer or from an exchange. A deposit needs the signed data from your file.

<When net="mainnet">

## Deposit through the launchpad

<Steps>

1. **Open the launchpad** (address above) and read through its checklist.

2. **Choose your clients.** Select Prysm and your execution client. This only tailors the launchpad's instructions.

3. **Upload `deposit_data-*.json`.** The launchpad checks the network, amounts, signatures, and that the keys weren't deposited before. If it rejects the file, don't work around it; the usual cause is keys made for another network.

4. **Connect your wallet** and check that it's on the same network as the launchpad.

5. **Send the deposit.** The launchpad creates one transaction per validator. In your wallet, check that each goes to the deposit contract above and that the amount matches (32 ETH for a standard validator).

6. **Keep the transaction hashes** the launchpad shows at the end.

</Steps>

</When>
<When net="hoodi">

## Deposit through the console

<Steps>

1. **Open the console** (address above) and read through its checklist.

2. **Upload `deposit_data-*.json`.** The console checks the network, amounts, signatures, and that the keys weren't deposited before. If it rejects the file, don't work around it; the usual cause is keys made for another network.

3. **Connect your wallet** and check that it's on the same network as the console.

4. **Send the deposit.** The console sends one transaction for all your validators. In your wallet, check that the total amount matches (32 ETH per standard validator).

5. **Keep the transaction hash** the console shows at the end.

</Steps>

</When>

## How long until it's active

After the deposit transaction is included, the beacon chain processes it, adds your validator, and then activates it.

<When net="mainnet">

- With no queue, on a healthy, finalizing network, this takes about 10 epochs (about an hour).
- When many people stake at once, deposits wait in a queue, which can take days or weeks.
- When the network doesn't finalize, which is extremely rare, deposits aren't processed and validators aren't activated until finality resumes.

</When>
<When net="hoodi">

- With no queue, on a healthy, finalizing network, this takes about 10 epochs (about an hour).
- When many people stake at once, deposits wait in a queue, which can take days or weeks.
- When the network doesn't finalize, deposits aren't processed and validators aren't activated until finality resumes.

</When>

<When>

You can see the current entry queue on a block explorer such as <a href="{explorer}">{"{explorer}"}</a>. Keep your node running the whole time: once active, your validator has duties from its first epoch.

</When>

## Check that it works

Ask your own beacon node about the validator:

<When os="unix">

```bash
curl -s http://127.0.0.1:3500/eth/v1/beacon/states/head/validators/<VALIDATOR_PUBKEY> | jq '.data.status'
```

</When>
<When os="win">

```powershell
curl.exe -s http://127.0.0.1:3500/eth/v1/beacon/states/head/validators/<VALIDATOR_PUBKEY>
```

</When>

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| None (the response says `Unknown validator`) | The deposit isn't processed yet. Wait. |
| `pending_initialized` or `pending_queued` | Deposit processed, waiting for activation. |
| `active_ongoing` | Active. Check that your validator attests. |
